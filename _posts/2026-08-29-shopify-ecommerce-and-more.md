## Introduction

Following on from choosing the e-commerce role over the traditional college route, this post is a first look at what that's actually meant day to day - starting with Shopify and the wider world of e-commerce.

## Getting Hands-On with Shopify

Shopify has been the main platform I've been learning through this role - from product listings and collections to the storefront theme and how the storefront connects to the backend. Coming from mostly self-taught HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, seeing how a production e-commerce platform structures all of this has been a genuinely useful step up.

Some of the areas I've been getting into:

- **Store setup and management** - products, collections, and inventory
- **Storefront customisation** - working with themes and layout
- **Orders and customers** - how a live store actually operates day to day
- **The bigger picture** - how design, development, and business decisions all connect on an e-commerce site

## Why This Fits My Wider Goals

This role ties together a few different threads I've been building on this site: front-end development fundamentals, an interest in building real products rather than just following tutorials, and now a much clearer picture of how e-commerce businesses actually work behind the scenes. It's practical experience I can bring directly into future web development and freelance work.

## What's Next

I plan to keep documenting what I learn here as I go deeper into Shopify and e-commerce more broadly, and to start applying some of it to my own concept projects - including the coffeeshop concept site already on my portfolio.

## Conclusion

A few weeks into this role, Shopify and e-commerce have already given me a much clearer, more practical view of web development than I had before. I'll keep this space updated as the "and more" part of the title starts to take shape.
