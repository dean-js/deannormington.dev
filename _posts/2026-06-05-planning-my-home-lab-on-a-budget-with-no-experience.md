## Introduction

This post is a working plan for building a home lab from scratch on a tight budget and with no prior experience. I want a practical space where I can learn networking, server management, and basic automation without spending a lot of money doing it.

## What I Want to Build

- A small home lab setup for learning and experimentation
- Basic networking with a router, switch, and a mix of wired and wireless devices
- One or more inexpensive machines for running Linux, containers, and small services
- A simple backup strategy, and an easy way to restore the system if something breaks

## Budget and Hardware

The plan is to keep the initial cost as low as possible by using second-hand equipment or repurposing older devices I already have, rather than buying anything new up front. Possible hardware includes:

- An old PC or laptop as the core lab machine
- A low-cost or managed switch for networking
- A Raspberry Pi for lightweight services or monitoring, tying in with my [Raspberry Pi projects](/projects/raspberry-pi-projects/)
- Existing routers and cabling already available at home

If none of that covers what I need, second-hand marketplaces are the next step before anything gets bought new.

## Skill Goals

My main learning goals for this project are:

- Understanding local networking and IP management properly, not just in theory
- Installing and managing Linux on a lab machine
- Running containers or virtual machines for small projects
- Creating a safe, isolated environment for testing, separate from anything that actually matters

## How This Fits the Rest of My Work

This sits alongside my [cyber security study](/projects/cybersecurity-projects/) rather than apart from it: a lab environment is where the NCFE and Cisco Academy theory actually gets tested against something real, safely and on hardware I already own.
